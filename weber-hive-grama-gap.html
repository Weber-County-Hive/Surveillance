The committees that could have closed this gap

Every audit-findings row above reads "not established" or "no historical forms produced" for a reason: a bill that would have fixed exactly that gap already passed both committees with jurisdiction over it — and still died.

House Transportation Committee
Approved HB 468 (Automatic License Plate Reader Amendments) 10–0–2 on Feb. 18, 2025. Unanimous.
House Floor
Passed HB 468 64–2 on Feb. 27, 2025. Overwhelming, bipartisan.
Senate Transportation Committee
Approved HB 468 6–0–2 on Mar. 4, 2025. Also unanimous.

HB 468 would have required every agency running these systems to submit standardized annual audit reports to the State Commission on Criminal and Juvenile Justice — a central, external body actually reading the audits, instead of an agency's own unlogged internal review. It would have set a hard 150-day statewide purge cap, in place of the patchwork retention rules currently in force. Both committees with substantive jurisdiction over ALPR legislation reviewed it and let it through without a single "no" vote.

The bill never got a final recorded floor vote in the Senate. At 11:59 p.m. on March 7, 2025 — one minute before the constitutional session deadline — the Senate struck the bill's enacting clause, killing it procedurally. No open debate, no recorded up-or-down vote on the substance. Who specifically moved that strike is not yet established in the record reviewed for this piece; that remains an open question worth a formal records request to the Senate Secretary's office.

This is not a one-off. A data-center transparency bill (S.B. 318, 2026) and an AI transparency bill (H.B. 286, 2026) were killed the same way, in the same window — sent to Senate Rules with days left in session, then struck at 11:59 p.m. on the final night, after committee approval. The lever that stops these bills isn't the committee that hears them on the merits. It's whatever happens after a bill leaves committee with support and reaches Senate Rules in the session's final days.

Primary Takeaway

Utah requires records connecting searches to crimes and incidents. The public production may not contain enough information to validate that those connections were ever made.

Crime type has no field to occupy in Flock's own platform, and incident number exists only as an unvalidated free-text field — both confirmed against Flock's documented export schema, not assumed from a summary.
