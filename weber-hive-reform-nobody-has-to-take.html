The Reform Nobody Has to Take

On August 13, 2026, Flock announced a sweeping set of privacy reforms for its ALPR network — new retention defaults, mandatory case codes, automated misuse detection. Read closely, one of the headline changes is optional. Nothing in the announcement requires any agency to actually adopt it.

What this article does not claim: nothing here says Weber County's retention period has changed, stayed the same, or is being reconsidered. That's an open question for a direct records request, not something this piece answers.

What Flock Actually Announced

A real reform package, confirmed industry-wide

This is not in dispute — Flock's own blog post and more than a dozen news outlets confirm the same set of changes, announced August 13, 2026.

  • A new 7-day default retention recommendation, down from 30 days, with a new “Evidence Mode” that lets detectives preserve specific records tied to an active investigation past that window
  • Mandatory case codes — officers must enter a code from their agency's own records management system before running a search, required for all law enforcement customers by January 1, 2027
  • Mandatory Audit Assistance — a machine-learning tool that flags abnormal search patterns for administrator review, optional since April 2026, becoming mandatory for all law enforcement customers by the end of 2026
  • Proactive lockouts — an account whose activity meets Flock's criteria for abnormal behavior automatically loses access pending administrator review
  • Offense-type filtering — a customer agency can now choose which categories of offense (homicide, arson, etc.) outside agencies are allowed to search its data for, which Flock says would let an agency block outside searches tied to immigration enforcement specifically
  • Security changes responsive to a November 2025 letter from Sen. Ron Wyden to the FTC flagging the lack of mandatory multi-factor authentication: Flock is now requiring MFA, commissioning an independent security review by Bishop Fox, and launching a Coordinated Vulnerability Disclosure program
The Detail That Matters

Not every reform in this package works the same way

Toggle between the two categories below. This is the single most important distinction in the whole announcement.

  • ✓Case codes required before a search runs — applies to all law enforcement customers by Jan. 1, 2027
  • ✓Audit Assistance activated — applies to all law enforcement customers by end of 2026
  • ✓Proactive lockout on flagged abnormal activity — tied to Audit Assistance, same mandatory rollout
  • ✓Multi-factor authentication — security requirement, applies broadly per Flock's announcement
  • ₀7-day default retention — new customers get this as the default; existing customers keep their current, previously approved retention period unless they choose to switch
  • ₀Offense-type filtering for outside searches — a capability an agency can choose to configure, not something applied automatically

Nothing in the public reporting on this announcement describes a mechanism for confirming which agencies have opted in to the shorter retention window and which haven't, or a public list of which posture each of Flock's thousands of law enforcement customers has chosen.

Two Paths, Same Announcement

What "Flock announced shorter retention" can actually mean for a given agency

Click each path. Both are fully consistent with the same headline.

Path A

Agency opts in

Switches to the new 7-day default, gains access to Evidence Mode for active-investigation exceptions.

Path B

Agency does nothing

Keeps whatever retention period it already had — 30 days, 60 days, or longer — with no requirement to announce that choice publicly.

Path A, in practice: The agency's retention window shortens to 7 days for ordinary captures. Detectives can still preserve specific records tied to an active case using Evidence Mode, so nothing about actual investigations is lost — only the default window for data nobody has flagged as relevant to anything narrows.
Path B, in practice: Nothing changes. The agency's retention period is whatever it was before August 13 — which, per multiple Flock ALPR audits reviewed elsewhere in this series, is very commonly still the original 30-day manufacturer default. The agency can describe itself as covered by Flock's "reformed" platform without having adopted the one reform that actually shortens how long ordinary location data is kept.
How Critics Read the Same Announcement

"Window dressing," or "a step in the right direction"?

Both reactions are on the record, from different vantage points.

ACLU
Called the shorter retention period "a step in the right direction" — while dismissing the other safeguards as "retreads" of inadequate protections.
Robert Frommer, Institute for Justice
Called the reforms "window dressing" from a company in "panic mode," arguing searches should instead require approval "by judges with real warrants."

The gap that survives the reform, per outside analysis

Even after case codes become mandatory in January 2027, nothing in the announced changes verifies that a code an officer enters actually corresponds to a real, open case. An officer can still type any case number into the required field. The mandatory field closes the "no reason given at all" gap this series has documented elsewhere — it does not close the "nobody checks if the reason is true" gap.

The Throughline

This is the same shape as every legislative reform this series has documented — real, mandatory pieces (case codes, audit tools, MFA) sitting next to an optional piece (the actual retention shortening) that gets described in headlines as if it applies to everyone. Whether Weber County has taken Path A or Path B isn't something this piece can answer. It's the next records request.
