Article 11 · Update, October 2026

What Changed Since September, and What Didn't

In the seven weeks after this series' last update, Weber County's commission approved a Flock contract amendment, a governor ordered a privacy review, two U.S. senators demanded answers, officers in four states were charged, and Flock changed its own defaults. None of those changes reaches the searches and copies other agencies have already made.

What changed in Weber County and Utah

Four public records, in the order they happened.

Feb. 17, 2026: the County Commission approved a Flock amendment

Consent item F9 on the Weber County Commission's Feb. 17, 2026 agenda: "Amend a contract with Flock Group Inc. for access of Flock's software and hardware situational awareness solution." The minutes record it approved with the rest of the consent items (Commissioner Bolos moved, Chair Froerer seconded). The minutes list no dollar amount, term, or quote number. They list Commissioner Harvey as excused, yet the consent vote line records "Commissioner Harvey – aye." The same meeting approved an Axon agreement for three Animal Control body cameras (item G5), a Securus inmate-phone amendment (F7) and a CALEA accreditation contract (G7).

Aug. 20, 2026: Gov. Cox ordered a privacy review

After Utah Civic Compact released data showing Weber County's 10 cameras had been searched more than 5 million times, mostly by out-of-state agencies, Gov. Spencer Cox said he was "deeply troubled" and asked the Utah Privacy Governing Board and Utah Privacy Commission to "do a review of all of our rules and regulations." He said: "If there are some gaps, then yes, I would absolutely support legislation." (KSL, Aug. 20, 2026)

Sept. 2, 2026: Washington Terrace paused its Flock trial

The city council voted unanimously to halt a planned trial. Mayor Mark Allen: "I believe they are a good tool for our law enforcement. But they do need to put some safeguards on those." Three cameras the Weber County Sheriff's Office runs inside the city remain in operation. (FOX 13, Sept. 2, 2026)

Sept. 17, 2026: a 2027 bill file was requested

Sen. Calvin Musselman, chair of the Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice Interim Committee, discussed a proposal "to strengthen privacy guardrails on automated license plate readers." The report says "the bill file was requested"; it does not name a primary sponsor or the bill's contents yet. (Utah News Dispatch, Sept. 17, 2026)

The Part That Matters Most

What didn't change

Flock's own changes cover Flock's own settings. Once another agency has searched or saved a record, that record follows the other agency's rules, not Utah's and not Flock's.

Changed

  • Flock's recommended default retention is 7 days, down from 30, for new law enforcement customers (Flock letter to Sen. Hawley, Sept. 8, 2026).
  • Flock says it removed federal agencies from its statewide and national lookup networks (August 2025) and added a one-click switch to turn off federal sharing (January 2026).
  • Flock says its "Audit Assistance" tool will be required for all police customers by the end of 2026.
  • Florida revoked approvals for local police plate readers on state highway right-of-way (Aug. 31, 2026). That order does not cover private property or roads the state doesn't control.

Didn't change

  • Existing customers "may keep their current retention period" (Flock, Sept. 8, 2026). Weber County's Flock quote lists 30 days; whether that setting has changed is not public.
  • Searches already run, and records already saved, by agencies outside Utah. 404 Media reported U.S. Customs and Border Protection had access to more than 80,000 Flock cameras during a 2025 pilot; Danville, Illinois records show more than 4,000 federal searches although ICE had no contract with that city.
  • Utah has no way to check what an out-of-state recipient does with Weber County data (see Article 2).
  • The commission's Feb. 17 amendment came before the July 1, 2027 date when new Utah privacy terms must be written into contractor agreements (see Article 6).

Your Turn

Questions to ask, and who can answer them

These are public questions any resident can bring to a public meeting or public comment period.

Weber County Commission
  • What did the Feb. 17, 2026 Flock amendment (item F9) change, and what does it cost?
  • Why do the minutes list Commissioner Harvey as excused and also record his vote?
  • Will the next Flock renewal include the privacy terms Utah will require from July 1, 2027?
Weber County Sheriff's Office
  • What retention setting does our Flock account use today: 30 days, 7 days, or something else?
  • Is federal sharing switched off in our account settings?
  • Has Flock's Audit Assistance tool been turned on, and has it flagged anything?
Your state legislators
  • Who is the primary sponsor of the 2027 ALPR bill file, and what will it contain?
  • Will it give Utah any way to check what out-of-state agencies do with Utah plate data?
  • What did the Privacy Governing Board's review, ordered Aug. 20, find?
The Throughline

A company can change its defaults overnight. It can't recall what has already left its servers. Every change in this update applies going forward, to the settings Flock and its customers control. The questions that remain are about the records that are already somewhere else.
